THE IDEA

A small vocabulary.
A finer spatial representation.

Multimodal large language models often express object locations as sequences of text or flat coordinate tokens. Increasing the resolution of a flat token representation requires a larger coordinate vocabulary.

HiLoc factorizes each coordinate into two levels. A Grid index selects a coarse region, and a Cell index refines the position within it. Both levels share the same index-token vocabulary. With 64 tokens per level, the representation encodes 4,096 discrete positions along each axis.

Built on Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, HiLoc combines supervised fine-tuning and GRPO post-training. HiRef-500K adds complex referring expressions and non-existence queries to support description grounding and rejection.

At equal resolution and with the same decoder, flat and hierarchical encodings have the same quantization error. HiLoc’s vocabulary advantage is representational; learned accuracy and optimization behavior are evaluated experimentally.

From coordinates to structured predictions.

HiLoc pairs phrase and box tags with Grid–Cell coordinates, allowing a single response to ground multiple queries and instances. Supervised fine-tuning teaches the output structure; GRPO then refines localization with task-specific rewards.

04 / ANALYSIS

Understanding the gains.

Representation, resolution, and post-training each play a role.

The ablations examine GRPO, coordinate formats, and training dynamics. Two controlled comparisons then separate the coordinate vocabulary from resolution and compare representations under shared data and reward settings.
